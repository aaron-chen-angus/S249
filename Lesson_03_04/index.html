LESSON 3 · FREQUENCY MEASURES

Ratio · Proportion · Rate

Compare one part of a distribution to another part, or to the whole.

Ratio

a : b

Compares any two values. a need not be part of b.

Proportion

aa + b

Part of the whole. Numerator is always inside the denominator. ×100 = %.

Rate

casespopulation × time

A measure of risk over time. Fair comparison of groups of different sizes.

WORKED EXAMPLES
PROPORTION

360 of 540 polyclinic attendees are women. Proportion of women?

Reveal working
360 ÷ 540 = 0.67
× 100% = 66.67%
RATIO

Same clinic. Ratio of men to women?

Reveal working
Men = 540 − 360 = 180
180 : 360 = 1 : 2
One man for every two women.
RATIO OF RATES

Singapore infant mortality: 1.499 (2021) vs 1.457 (2022) per 1,000 live births. Ratio 2021 : 2022?

Reveal working
1.499 ÷ 1.457 = 1.03 : 1
2021's rate was 1.03 times as high.
RATE

An Active Ageing Centre follows 2,400 seniors for one year; 96 have a fall-related ED visit. Rate?

Reveal working
96 ÷ 2,400 = 0.04 per senior per year
× 1,000 = 40 per 1,000 seniors per year

Q2 · Gastroenteritis outbreak

At a corporate dinner, 99 people ate potato salad; 66 developed gastroenteritis.

Q2 (i)

Proportion of potato-salad eaters who developed gastroenteritis? (2 d.p.)

Proportion = cases ÷ all who ate potato salad
= 66 ÷ 99 = 0.67 (66.67%)
Q2 (ii)

Among potato-salad eaters, ratio of those with to those without gastroenteritis? (format a:b)

Without = 99 − 66 = 33
66 : 33 = 2 : 1
Twice as many fell ill as did not.
LESSON 4 · NEW CASES

Incidence

How fast are people developing the disease?

INCIDENCE=NEW cases in a periodpopulation AT RISK

At risk = disease-free people (including those who have recovered).

Cumulative incidence

new casespopulation initially at risk

A risk (proportion) over a defined period.

Incidence rate

new casesperson-time at risk

Accounts for different follow-up times. Mostly used in research.

CASE FATALITY

fatal cases ÷ all cases

CUMULATIVE MORTALITY

fatal cases ÷ initially at risk

PROPORTIONAL MORTALITY

deaths from disease ÷ all deaths

DEATH RATE

deaths ÷ population, per 1,000 per year

WORKED 1 · DIABETES COHORT

10,000 healthy people followed 1 Jan – 31 Dec. Those lost to follow-up (FU) were disease-free when lost.

WORKED 2

10,000 men aged 50+ screened for prostate cancer. New cases: 2015 → 2 · 2016 → 2 · 2017 → 3 · 2018 → 2. Cumulative incidence 2016–2018?

Reveal working
At risk at start of 2016 = 10,000 − 2 = 9,998
New cases = 2 + 3 + 2 = 7
7 ÷ 9,998 = 0.0007 (0.07%)
WORKED 3 · PERSON-TIME

Five nursing-home residents followed up to 12 months for a first fall.

Reveal working
Person-time = 12 + 6 + 4 + 9 + 12 = 43 person-months
2 falls ÷ 43 = 0.047 per person-month
× 12 × 100 = ≈ 56 falls per 100 person-years

LESSON 4 · EXISTING CASES

Prevalence

How much disease is in the population right now?

PREVALENCE=ALL existing cases (old + new)total population

Point prevalence

existing cases on a datepopulation on that date

A snapshot.

Period prevalence

cases at any time in a periodestimated population in the period

A time-lapse.

WORKED EXAMPLES
POINT

5,000 students; 50 have flu today.

Reveal
50 ÷ 5,000 = 0.01 (1%)
PERIOD

Same 5,000 students; 120 had flu last week.

Reveal
120 ÷ 5,000 = 0.024 (2.4%)
SG COMMUNITY CARE

A polyclinic screens 800 seniors on one day; 240 have diabetes.

Reveal
240 ÷ 800 = 0.30 (30%) point prevalence
