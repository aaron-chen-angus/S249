LESSON 5 · STEPS IN A POPULATION HEALTH STUDY

From Question to Conclusion

01 TWO OPPOSING HYPOTHESES
H₀
NULL HYPOTHESIS

The default. No difference, no relationship.

H₁
ALTERNATIVE HYPOTHESIS

The opposite of H₀. What we expect to find.

EXAMPLE · COOKIES

The pack says 100 g. You suspect it is less.

Reveal hypotheses
H₀: The net weight is 100 g.
H₁: The net weight is less than 100 g (or: is NOT 100 g).
EXAMPLE · MATHS TEST

Are Engineering students better at Maths than Science students?

Reveal hypotheses
H₀: Engineering students' Maths performance is NOT significantly different from Science students'.
H₁: It IS significantly different (or: Engineering students perform significantly better).
02 THE P-VALUE DECIDES
DRAG THE P-VALUE0.030

p = the chance of seeing a difference this big if H₀ were true.

p < 0.05 → REJECT H₀ · SIGNIFICANTp ≥ 0.05 → ACCEPT H₀ · NOT SIGNIFICANT

"ACCEPT H₀" IS ALSO CALLED "FAIL TO REJECT H₀"

    02 CHANCE vs BIAS

    Selection bias

    From how people are chosen.

    Example
    Door-to-door survey in the daytime finds more disease in the elderly and unemployed – who is home in the day?

    Information bias

    From wrong measurement or recall.

    Example
    Mothers of babies with heart defects remember their medicine use in pregnancy better than other mothers (recall bias).

    Confounding

    A third factor mixed up with the exposure.

    Example
    Inactivity and heart disease in seniors – but age drives both.

    A confounder is linked to both the exposure and the outcome.

    CALC COHORT · RELATIVE RISK
    DISEASEDNOT DISEASEDTOTAL

    UNEXPOSED

    Example: 1,000 inactive vs 1,000 active seniors, followed 2 years for a fall injury. ILLUSTRATIVE

    RISK · EXPOSED = a ÷ (a+b)
    RISK · UNEXPOSED = c ÷ (c+d)
    RR = risk exp ÷ risk unexp
    AR = Ie − Iu (per 1,000)
    AR% = AR ÷ Ie × 100
    CALC CASE-CONTROL · ODDS RATIO
    CASESCONTROLS
    EXPOSED
    NOT EXPOSED

    Example: 100 seniors with hip fracture (cases) vs 200 without (controls). Exposure = sleeping pills. ILLUSTRATIVE

    Odds in cases = a ÷ c
    Odds in controls = b ÷ d
    OR = (a÷c) ÷ (b÷d)

    No incidence in a case-control study – so risk is estimated with the OR (good for rare diseases).

    RR

    Strength of association · causes (aetiology) · cohort.

    OR

    Estimates risk · case-control · rare diseases. Adjusted OR (AOR) controls confounders.

    AR / AR%

    How much disease is due to the exposure – and could be prevented · planning.
